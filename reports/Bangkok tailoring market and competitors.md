# Magick Tailor must beat LINE, not software

Magick Tailor's real competitor in Bangkok is not another tailoring app but the free stack every shop already runs: LINE chat, a PromptPay QR, a slip screenshot and a paper order book. No tailor-specific software has a foothold in Thailand. The only Thai-made tailor program found is a Windows desktop tool last updated in 2017, and the iOS tailor apps that exist have between zero and seven ratings each. The market splits into two very different customer types. One is a few hundred tourist-belt suit shops on Sukhumvit, Silom, Siam and Banglamphu, often Indian-Thai family sales fronts selling suits from about 6,000 to 40,000 THB. The other is a much larger, mostly invisible layer of Thai neighbourhood dressmakers and alteration shops that earn around 30,000 THB a month. Magick Tailor's features cover workflow gaps no competitor fills together: measurement history with a fit diff, Thai voice dictation, metre-based fabric deduction, deposit receipts, referral links and review requests. Three things in the current build hold it back: an English-only interface, an iOS-only release in a market where roughly 26–36% of phones run iOS, and a free tier capped at three clients. The recommended positioning is "the trust-and-repeat-business system for reputable tourist and expat tailors, built to sit beside LINE," with a Thai interface as the gate to the larger domestic segment. Almost every number about how shops actually work today (tools, order volume, deposit habits, willingness to pay) is still unmeasured and needs interviews with tailors.

**How to read this report.** Facts with an inline citation come from the research notes behind this report. Sentences that begin with "Inference" or "We infer," and all of the recommendations, are this report's own reasoning. Statements about Magick Tailor's features come from a direct check of the repository (`mobile/app`, `mobile/api`, `CHANGELOG.md`) on 2026-10-26.

## Two markets share one city: tourist suit fronts and Thai dressmakers

No official count of Bangkok tailor shops exists. The most quoted figure, **about 5,000 "documented" tailoring businesses in the capital**, is a 2015 opinion estimate, not a registry count ([Parisian Gentleman](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/q-a-with-pg-editors-should-i-buy-a-suit-from-a-bangkok-tailor)). The visible, English-language trade clusters on **Sukhumvit between Nana and Asok** (Soi 8, 10, 11, 45), plus Silom, Charoenkrung, Thonglor, MBK in Siam, and Phra Athit near Khao San ([Time Out Bangkok](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/shopping/the-best-tailor-shops-in-bangkok)). The Sikh and Punjabi textile-trading district of Phahurat is the historical fabric supply base ([Wikipedia: Phahurat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phahurat)). We infer that the tourist belt holds a few hundred storefronts. If the 5,000 figure means anything, it must include neighbourhood alteration stalls, market seamstresses and ห้องเสื้อ dressmakers, a layer that barely appears in English sources.

The segments are priced far apart. A Bangkok tailor's own published price tiers run **8,000–15,000 THB for budget made-to-measure, 15,000–30,000 THB for mid-range half-canvas, and 30,000–70,000 THB for full-canvas bespoke**, with made-to-measure taking 2–4 weeks and 1–2 fittings ([SUITCUBE](https://www.suitcube.com/en/how-much-does-a-tailored-suit-cost-in-thailand/)). Tourist-belt shops advertise suits **from 6,000–8,000 THB in 12 hours to 3 days** ([Time Out Bangkok](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/shopping/the-best-tailor-shops-in-bangkok)). A self-interested shop listicle puts ten well-known shops at 7,000–40,000+ THB ([besttailorbangkok.com](https://besttailorbangkok.com/best-tailors-in-bangkok/)). The domestic market works at a different scale. A provincial dressmaker making formal wear and chut Thai **charges from 1,800 THB and earns about 30,000 THB a month** ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9660000088939)). Simple alterations cost **60–100 THB** (Chiang Mai data) ([Chiang Mai CityLife](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/citynews/general/cost-of-school-uniforms-lead-to-long-tailoring-queues/)). Inference: one subscription price has to make sense both to a shop selling 20,000–50,000 THB tourist orders and to one selling 1,000–5,000 THB local orders, and it cannot.

The two segments also operate differently. On Sukhumvit, "most of the shops are sales fronts where no actual stitching happens on-site" ([Superfuture forum](https://supertalk.superfuture.com/topic/120967-best-tailor-in-bangkok/)). They are often multi-generation family businesses that keep **measurements on file for remote reorders over WhatsApp** and ship worldwide (Woolrich, Om Fashion, Perfect Tailor, Tailored By Luis) ([Time Out Bangkok](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/shopping/the-best-tailor-shops-in-bangkok); [Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/guides/best-of/lifestyle/woolrich-bespoke-tailor-bangkok)). The reputable trade wins customers through search, hotel concierges, sponsored articles, bloggers with promo codes and trunk shows abroad. The "package" trade wins them through touts and tuk-tuk commission steering ([Thailand Insider Guide](https://thailandinsiderguide.com/?p=4522); [Khaosod English sponsored](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/sponsored/2026/05/22/the-best-tailor-in-bangkok-perfect-tailor-bkk/); [Wah So Shiok](https://www.wahsoshiok.com/narry-bespoke-tailors-bangkok-review-promo-code-included/)). Domestic dressmakers market in **Facebook bridal and formal-wear groups and take bookings by phone**, with no software mentioned ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9660000088939)).

Trust is the trade's main currency. The "Thai tailor scam" is a well-documented confidence trick: a tout, a supposed export-company sale, full payment upfront, then polyester delivered in place of cashmere ([Wikipedia: Thai tailor scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_tailor_scam)). Travel guides tell visitors to **pay only a deposit, get the price in writing, keep fabric samples and attend fittings** ([Thailand Insider Guide](https://thailandinsiderguide.com/?p=4522); [AFF forum](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/good-tailor-in-bangkok.27762/latest)). Online ratings barely separate shops: listed Google ratings cluster at **4.4–5.0** ([besttailorbangkok.com](https://besttailorbangkok.com/best-tailors-in-bangkok/)). Narry alone shows **615 TripAdvisor reviews** ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g293916-d2523915-Reviews-Narry_Tailors-Bangkok.html)). Inference: when every shop sits near 4.8, review volume and recency, plus visible proof of honesty, decide who a tourist picks.

### Tourism is shrinking at the top of the funnel

The tourist segment is under pressure. **International arrivals fell 7.2% in 2025 to 32.9 million**, the first non-pandemic fall in a decade, and Chinese arrivals dropped about a third ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/tourism/2025/12/11/thailand-tourist-arrivals-fall-to-32m-amid-challenging-2025/)). January–September 2026 arrivals were **23.2 million, down another 3.8%**, with India now the third-largest source market at 1.68 million ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/tourism/40071711)). Domestic trips grew 2.7% in 2025 ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/thailand-tourism-slips-in-2025-despite-domestic-growth/)). Inference: fewer walk-ins make each returning client, remote reorder and referral worth more to a tourist shop, which suits Magick Tailor's retention features. The same decline also squeezes those shops' budgets for new subscriptions.

## The incumbent costs nothing: LINE, PromptPay and paper

**LINE has 56 million Thai users, more than Facebook's 51 million** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-thailand)). A LINE Official Account is **free for up to 300 broadcast messages a month**, and paid plans cost **1,280–1,780 THB a month** ([LINE for Business TH](https://www.lineforbusiness.com/th/service/line-oa-features/broadcast-message)). One-to-one chat has no quota, so we infer that most micro-shops never pay LINE anything. LINE also has a light CRM, but the free tier allows only **one tag and one note per chat** ([LINE OA help](https://help2.line.me/official_account_th/web/categoryId/20006370/pc?lang=en)). That is where a tailor might jot down measurements today, and it cannot hold a structured measurement set, let alone a history of them. Booking comes only from agency-built add-ons, not stock LINE ([Cipher](https://www.cipher.co.th/en/line-official-account)).

Payment habits are just as settled. **PromptPay has more than 81 million registered users** ([Juspay](https://juspay.io/en-sg/blog/promptpay-reshaping-thailand-s-financial-landscape)). Since **30 October 2025, Chinese tourists can pay Thai QR codes with Alipay, WeChat Pay and UnionPay** ([Bank of Thailand](https://www.bot.or.th/en/news-and-media/news/news-20251030.html)). Tourist tailors typically take a **50% deposit at the first fitting** ([Nathan Tailors](https://www.nathantailors.com/zh/tailoring/bangkok-vs-hong-kong)). Shops with turnover under **1.8 million THB a year do not have to register for VAT** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/value-added-tax-vat-in-thailand)), so a simple receipt (ใบเสร็จรับเงิน) is enough for most small tailors.

Inference: the incumbent workflow is a paper order book or measurement card, a LINE chat with photos of fabric and the written form, a PromptPay slip screenshot as proof of deposit, and a LINE or phone reminder before the fitting. No source confirms it, and this is the single most important thing for interviews to check. The stack costs nothing and the customer already has it installed. A Thai SME owner's software price anchors are **free (Loyverse, LINE OA, FlowAccount Free), about 165–249 THB a month (FlowAccount), and about 600–990 THB a month (ZORT, Ocha, SeeU salon POS)** ([FlowAccount](https://flowaccount.com/en/mobile-pos); [ZORT](https://zortout.com/en/package); [SeeU comparison, vendor-written](https://seeuapp.io/en/for-business/blog/salon-pos-price-thailand)). Appetite to spend is low: a UTCC survey found **about 92% of SMEs had no plan to invest in digital technology** ([Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/business/general/2960796/small-firms-shun-digital-investment)). Every leading Thai SME tool sells in Thai first ([LINE for Business TH](https://www.lineforbusiness.com/th/service/line-oa-features/broadcast-message); [Ocha on App Store TH](https://apps.apple.com/th/app/ocha-pos/id1144791811)).

Inference: Magick Tailor cannot replace LINE and should not try. It wins only by adding the structure LINE lacks (measurement records, order stages, deposits owed, fittings, fabric) while sending every customer-facing output back into LINE. The current build already does part of this: receipts and referral links go out through the share sheet to LINE or WhatsApp, and client records have a LINE ID field (`shared/types.ts`).

## Tailor software is fragmented, foreign and barely used in Thailand

The software market splits into four groups, and none is established in Thailand. The first is Western work-order SaaS at **US$29–199 a month with trials instead of free tiers**. Orderry is the closest feature match, covering measurement history, stages, deposits, fabric inventory and SMS ([Orderry](https://orderry.com/for-atelier/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/151174/Orderry/pricing/); [GarmentDesk](https://garmentdesk.com/)). The second is Indian cloud ERPs and apps at **₹200–1,000 a month or US$480+ a year**, such as Atelierware with automatic WhatsApp reminders and a claimed 700+ shops ([Atelierware](https://www.atelierware.com/); [Techjockey TailorPad](https://techjockey.com/detail/tailorpad)). The third is many Android "measurement book" apps with 10K–50K installs ([Google Play, DarzeeBook](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.darzeebook.darzee&hl=en)). The fourth is a scattering of iOS-native apps with almost no ratings. The Thai-specific options are an abandoned **Windows program last updated in 2017 at 4,990 THB** ([Thaiware](https://www.thaiware.com/software/11326/)) and cloud tools built for **dress-rental** shops rather than tailors, such as RentSabai at 5,000–7,500 THB a year with LINE OA support and a claimed 500+ Thai shops ([RentSabai](https://rentsabai.com/)).

On the evidence gathered, **no tailor product combines measurement history, deposits, fabric inventory and a referral or review feature**. Voice-dictated measurements in Thai, a before-and-after fit diff, and fabric deduction at cutting appear in none of the listings checked ([Orderry](https://orderry.com/for-atelier/); [Dress Measurement](https://dressmeasurement.com/); [TailorFlow](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tailorflow/id6741868343)). ## Positioning: the trust-and-repeat-business system that sits beside LINE

The recommendation is to launch for the **reputable tourist and expat-facing tailor**: shops on Sukhumvit, Silom and Thonglor, often Indian-Thai owned, that sell 6,000–40,000 THB suits to foreigners and depend on reviews and reorders. We then treat a Thai interface as the gate to the larger domestic segment. This order follows from the product as it stands. Its English interface fits this segment, where staff already work in English and WhatsApp. Its iOS-only build fits too, since we infer that owners of these shops skew toward iPhones, though no source confirms it. And its strongest features map directly onto this segment's documented problems. Deposit receipts that name the fabric, balance due and reference number, progress photos tagged by stage, and a fitting timeline turn the travel-guide advice ("pay only a deposit, get it in writing, check the fabric") into something the shop can show the customer ([Thailand Insider Guide](https://thailandinsiderguide.com/?p=4522)). Measurement history makes "measurements on file for reorders" a demonstrable promise rather than a marketing claim ([Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/guides/best-of/lifestyle/woolrich-bespoke-tailor-bangkok)). Google review requests target the deciding factor when every shop sits at 4.5–5.0. Smart Referral formalises the trade's existing incentive, a free shirt with a suit, which today travels only through bloggers' promo codes ([Wah So Shiok](https://www.wahsoshiok.com/narry-bespoke-tailors-bangkok-review-promo-code-included/)). Evidence from a German bank study found referred customers worth **16–25% more over their lifetime** ([Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Schmitt-Skiera-vandenBulte-2011-Referral-Programs-Customer-Value.pdf)). That study is old and from another industry, so it is suggestive only.

The pitch should not be "tailor shop software," because a shop will compare that with free. It should be "look like the honest shop, and get the client back." Against Orderry and GarmentDesk, Magick Tailor is a quarter of the price, phone-native and tailor-specific. Against Dress Measurement, it is cheaper and adds fabric, referrals and reviews. Against LINE, it is the structured record that LINE's single note per chat cannot be, and it still delivers through LINE.

The domestic Thai dressmaker is the larger long-term prize, but the current product does not fit it. That segment earns about 30,000 THB a month ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9660000088939)), works in Thai, and mostly uses Android phones. At about 330 THB, Pro would cost roughly 1% of revenue. Inference: winning that segment needs a Thai UI, Android and a usable free tier, all three together.

## Five threats could stall adoption before features matter

**Thailand runs mostly on Android.** StatCounter puts iOS at **26.01% of Thai mobile traffic in September 2026** and 31–36% in other recent months and datasets ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/thailand)). An iOS-only v1 cannot reach about two-thirds of phones, and the excluded share is probably concentrated among the Thai dressmakers in the domestic segment. This threat is acceptable for a tourist-shop beachhead and fatal for a mass-market play. Android has to come before any domestic push.

**LINE is the incumbent and could move into the gap.** LINE is building a business stack with MyShop chat commerce, more than 1,000 MINI Apps, and MyTeam planned for early 2027 ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/digital-marketing-en/line-thailand-business-solutions-2026/)). Inference: a LINE MINI App for appointments or measurement cards, from LINE or an agency, would compete with Magick Tailor inside the app every client already uses. The defence is depth that a chat add-on is unlikely to build (fit diffs, fabric metres, order stages) and integrating with LINE rather than competing for the chat.

**Under the PDPA, Magick Tailor is a data processor with its own liability.** In a 2025 case, an online reservation-system breach of 200,000 records brought a **3 million THB fine for the data processor against 500,000 THB for the controller**. Total PDPA fines have passed **21 million THB**, and enforcement applies "regardless of organizational type or size" ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/09/thailand-pdpa-crackdown-2025-are-you-next-major-fines-and-lessons-from-thailands-latest-enforcement/); [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/more-than-a-warning-eight-serious-fines-imposed-in-thai-data-protection-cases/)). Health or disability notes count as sensitive data that needs explicit consent ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/th/pdf/2022/06/legal-news-flash-issue-14.pdf)), and a tailor's fit note about a post-surgery fit could easily contain one. The privacy policy already names the shop as controller and Magick Tailor as processor. It also states that client records are stored in **Tokyo (Turso)** and order photos in Bangkok (`mobile/docs/privacy-policy.md`). Storing records in Tokyo is a cross-border transfer that needs stated safeguards. The repository shows no consent capture when a client is created, no published data processing agreement, and no breach procedure.

**Tourism decline shrinks the beachhead.** Arrivals fell 7.2% in 2025 and another 3.8% in 2026 to date ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/tourism/2025/12/11/thailand-tourist-arrivals-fall-to-32m-amid-challenging-2025/); [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/tourism/40071711)). Fewer tourists means fewer shops willing to pay, and a possible shakeout among the very shops being targeted. The mitigating shift is that Indian, Malaysian and returning long-haul visitors are growing in share, and those clients value remote reorders.

**The free tier is too small to create habits.** Competing mobile apps offer usable free tiers (Tailored Made, Dress Measurement), and every Thai price anchor starts at free ([Dress Measurement](https://dressmeasurement.com/); [Loyverse via Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/150632/Loyverse-POS/)). One shop alone carries hundreds of reviews and takes corporate orders of 20+ suits ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g293916-d2523915-Reviews-Narry_Tailors-Bangkok.html); [Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/guides/best-of/lifestyle/woolrich-bespoke-tailor-bangkok)). Inference: a three-client cap ends the trial within a day, before the measurement history, fit diff or referral loop has shown any value. Those features only pay off on a client's second visit. With 92% of SMEs reporting no plan to invest in digital tools, a product that asks for money before it shows value will lose to free.

## Concrete moves for product and pricing

**Product, in priority order.** First, add a **Thai interface** with Thai dates (including optional Buddhist-era years), a nickname (ชื่อเล่น) field that client search also matches, and Thai phone formatting. Today only the voice dictation, the ฿ formatting and the inch-to-centimetre conversion are Thai-aware. Screens, receipts and reports are in English, and the repository has no i18n library. Second, add a **PromptPay QR on the deposit receipt** and record the **payment method** (cash, PromptPay with slip photo, card, Alipay/WeChat via Thai QR). The `order_payments` table records only the amount and date. PromptPay QR is an open EMVCo standard that can be generated from the shop's phone number or tax ID, so this feature costs nothing to run. Third, make **"send to LINE" the default action** on every output (receipt, fitting reminder, "your suit is ready," referral link), and add an optional bilingual receipt so a tourist shop can serve both Thai and foreign clients. Fourth, close the **PDPA gaps**: a ready-made Thai privacy notice with a consent checkbox when a client is created, a separate field marked "health or fit-sensitive" that requires explicit consent, a client-level export and delete, a published data processing agreement, and a stated cross-border transfer safeguard for the Tokyo database. Fifth, market the trust features by name: put "fabric named on receipt, progress photos, deposit only" in the App Store copy and the shop-facing pitch. Sixth, keep the review request **neutral and unincentivised**. Google forbids gating or rewarding reviews, and the Bangkok listicle space is already visibly gamed ([besttailorbangkok.com](https://besttailorbangkok.com/best-tailors-in-bangkok/)). Treat Android as the prerequisite for any domestic-segment push, not a nice-to-have.

**Pricing.** The free tier should be redefined around **active orders or monthly volume**, not total clients. For example, the free plan could allow unlimited client files with **up to 10–15 active orders**, with receipts, reports, referrals and fabric stock behind Pro. That lets a shop live in the app for a full month and hit the "changes since last fitting" moment on its own clients. Pro at **US$9.99 (about 330 THB)** sits in the lower middle of Thai SME software (165–990 THB) and below Dress Measurement's $12.99, so it holds. It should be **priced in baht on the Thai App Store** (for example ฿299 or ฿349) with an annual option near the 5,000–7,500 THB a year that RentSabai's Thai customers already pay ([RentSabai](https://rentsabai.com/)). Premium at **US$24.99 (about 825 THB)** competes with Ocha, ZORT POS and SeeU, which come with Thai sales and support teams ([SeeU comparison](https://seeuapp.io/en/for-business/blog/salon-pos-price-thailand)). It needs a clearly different purpose: multi-staff accounts, a workshop hand-off view for sales-front shops that outsource sewing, multiple branches, and bulk corporate orders. Otherwise it should wait until those features exist. A depa listing could later unlock the **10,000 THB voucher and 200% tax deduction** for juristic-person shops ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/how-smes-benefit-from-the-200-tax-deduction)). That requires a Thai legal entity, and most micro-tailors are sole proprietors who would not qualify.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "which tailor app do we beat?" to "what will make a shop open a second app beside LINE?" No incumbent product exists to displace. The barrier is the habit of a free, good-enough workflow and an SME base that mostly does not buy software. That makes the free-tier design and the LINE hand-off more important to Magick Tailor's chances than any single new feature. The features most likely to drive adoption are the ones that produce something a shop's customer sees, such as a receipt, a progress photo or a referral link, because those turn the app into a visible trust signal in a trade with a scam reputation.

The tourist-tailor beachhead fits the product as built today, but it is a shrinking, few-hundred-shop niche exposed to tourism cycles. The durable business is the Thai domestic dressmaker, and reaching it depends on three things the roadmap does not yet have: a Thai interface, an Android build and a free tier that works for a real month. The next steps should be the interviews first, then Thai localisation and PromptPay, and then a revised free tier and pricing in baht, all before the planned prices are coded.
